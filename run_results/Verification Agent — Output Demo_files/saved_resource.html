Sample outputs from three verification scenarios — a PayPal phishing message, an unsourced health claim, and a BBC news report — showing how the four-block response contract renders for each risk level.

› URGENT: Your PayPal account has been suspended. Click now to verify your account or it will be permanently closed.
🔴 High Risk
91% confidence

This message has serious reliability concerns — it uses urgent time-pressure language demanding immediate action on a suspended account. Do not click any links or provide personal information until manually verified.

Why

  1. 1Urgent, fear-based language is present — "URGENT", "suspended", and "permanently closed" are classic phishing pressure tactics designed to bypass rational judgement.
  2. 2No identifiable or verifiable source — the message claims to be from PayPal but provides no cryptographic signature, display name, or sender domain that can be verified.
  3. 3Credential harvesting is being attempted — this pattern is consistent with a phishing message targeting account login details or payment information.

Next Step

Do not click any links. If you are concerned about your PayPal account, open a new browser tab and go directly to paypal.com. Never log in via a link from a message.

⚠ Needs Human Verification — do not act or share until reviewed
› Blog post: "Scientists say daily coffee reduces Alzheimer's risk by 65%" — no author named, cites one study with no link.
🟡 Medium Risk
72% confidence

This content has some reliability concerns — no named author or institutional affiliation is present, and the specific percentage claim rests on a single unnamed study. Verify with a primary source before acting or sharing.

Why

  1. 1No author or publisher is identifiable — bold health statistics published anonymously are a common pattern in health misinformation.
  2. 2The claim cannot be fully cross-checked: a single study rarely justifies a precise percentage-point health claim without independent peer replication.
  3. 3Evidence quality is weak — specific statistical figures without a linked or named source reference are a recognised misinformation signal.

Next Step

Search for this claim on nhs.uk or a peer-reviewed database such as PubMed. Check whether multiple independent studies support the figure before sharing with others.

› BBC News article reporting ONS figures: UK inflation fell to 2.1% in June, down from 2.3% in May.
🟢 Low Risk
88% confidence

This content appears broadly reliable based on the checks performed. The BBC is an established, independently verified public broadcaster and the claim references a primary ONS statistical release. Always verify important facts with an official source.

Why

  1. 1The source is a well-established news organisation with clear editorial accountability, an identifiable publisher, and a long publication history.
  2. 2The claim references a primary statistical source — the Office for National Statistics — which can be independently verified at ons.gov.uk.
  3. 3Language is factual and measured: no urgency pressure, fear tactics, or personal information requests are present.

Next Step

This looks reliable. To verify the figures directly, visit ons.gov.uk and search for the latest CPI bulletin, or check the BBC article's linked ONS reference.
